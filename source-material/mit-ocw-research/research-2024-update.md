# MIT OCW 18.642, Fall 2024: verified update to 18.S096

Research checked against MIT OCW on September 25–26, 2026. This report maps all 15 weekly pages and the numbered calendar, then examines selected substantive PDFs. A follow-up inspected the raw public HTML of the lecture 18 and 20 video resources for PDF transcripts and caption tracks: lecture 18 provides both, and its transcript was read; lecture 20 supplies an empty transcript-link attribute and no transcript/caption file link. No videos were downloaded. Exact lecture titles were also checked against the calendar's HTML. This report distinguishes scheduled lectures from publicly available learning material. Assignment requirements are covered separately by the main research report. PDF references use displayed slide/page numbers where possible.

MIT explicitly identifies 18.642 as the updated version of Fall 2013's 18.S096 and encourages learners to consult the earlier offering for additional materials. The teaching model still alternates mathematical foundations with industry applications. The 2024 public package combines lecture recordings, slides, mathematical notes, R examples, and a Python notebook. It should be treated as a complementary successor rather than assumed to contain every specialized subject from 2013. [2024 course overview](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-642-topics-in-mathematics-with-applications-in-finance-fall-2024/)

## What the substantive additions actually teach

### Machine learning: pricing and sequential hedging

Hull's notes distinguish learning from routine automation, introduce unsupervised, supervised and reinforcement learning, and explain training/validation/test separation and overfitting. Neural-network topics include activation functions, gradient descent, scaling, learning rates and backpropagation. The pricing example learns noisy Black-Scholes-Merton call prices; the slides also describe constructing fast approximations to expensive exotic-option valuations. A second example predicts changes in implied volatility using option moneyness, maturity and underlying returns, with VIX added as an environmental feature. Reinforcement learning introduces states, actions, rewards, exploration, and Q-value updates through Nim, then deep Q-learning. The closing application compares sequential hedging with conventional Greek-based methods. Reported advantages include transaction-cost savings and flexible risk objectives; the slides also acknowledge higher computational demands. These are teaching examples and the lecturer's reported findings, not independently validated trading results. [Hull's full slides, especially 8–10, 13–27 and 29–39](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-642-topics-in-mathematics-with-applications-in-finance-fall-2024/mit18_642_f24_lec23.pdf)

### Counterparty optimization: from risk measurement to implementation

Shepherd develops derivatives risk, netting, collateral and central clearing, then coherent risk measures, VaR/expected shortfall, initial margin, and network optimization. The material includes ISDA SIMM and sensitivity-based approximation. Its practical importance is the transition from measuring an isolated portfolio to reducing aggregate margin while satisfying each participant's constraints. Examples address FX non-deliverable forwards and interest-rate exposures. Numerical considerations include convexity, sparse matrix structure, conditioning, continuous solutions followed by integer rounding, trade counts and allocation fairness. Thus the addition joins probability, linear algebra and optimization with real operational constraints. It is more specific than a general lecture about counterparty defaults: the intended outcome is a feasible trade network with improved margin use. [Counterparty optimization PDF, 52 pages](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-642-topics-in-mathematics-with-applications-in-finance-fall-2024/mit18_642_f24_lec10.pdf)

### PCA: an empirical strategy bridge

Andreev's notes derive the covariance/eigenvector/SVD connection and discuss demeaning, optional variance normalization and rolling estimation windows. The Treasury-yield example examines in-sample versus out-of-sample factor behavior and instability near crises. Principal components become tradeable portfolios, including curve steepeners and butterflies neutral to selected factors. The material explains why residual portfolios can offer additional opportunities but face transaction costs, changing weights, leverage and financing constraints. It introduces effective portfolio dimensionality and an illustrative mean-reversion signal delayed by one day for actionability. Suggested investigations include combining butterflies, carry/rolldown and changing the covariance lookback. The public notebook and data make this a particularly useful route from an abstract decomposition to an empirical finance experiment. [PCA slides, especially 10–25](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-642-topics-in-mathematics-with-applications-in-finance-fall-2024/mit18_642_f24_lec09.pdf)

### Rates updated for market practice

The linear-rates slides cover bonds, swaps and futures, with funding, clearing and margining complicating apparently simple products. They discuss LIBOR's weaknesses and SOFR-based overnight secured rates, distinct discounting and index curves, fixed/floating swap cash flows and yield-curve construction. Later sections connect curve sensitivities to hedging and P&L attribution, electronic price-making, duration and z-spreads. This is useful current-market context within a 2024 teaching artifact; historical market-size figures in the slides should retain their cited dates rather than be repeated as today's statistics. [Linear-rates slides](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-642-topics-in-mathematics-with-applications-in-finance-fall-2024/mit18_642_f24_lec07.pdf)

### Portfolio management revisited

Xia's updated deck retains the endowment and portfolio-construction viewpoint, including return objectives, liquidity, loss tolerance, time horizon, leverage and risk parity. It expressly emphasizes limitations of mean-variance optimization: unreliable inputs, unstable solutions, and volatility's failure to distinguish upside from downside. Later material discusses expected gain and loss for comparing and sizing investments, the repeatability of historical patterns, interacting agents, crowd synchronization and the emergence of power-law distributions. Sections covering the lecturer's research should be read as proposed models and critiques rather than universal replacements for established portfolio theory. Portfolio management itself is inherited from 2013; these later crowding/power-law sections are the visible update emphasis. [Portfolio slides, dated October 22, 2024](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-642-topics-in-mathematics-with-applications-in-finance-fall-2024/mit18_642_f24_lec13.pdf)

## Mathematical continuity and refreshed practical exercises

The early-market notes formalize one-period economies, portfolio payoffs, replicating contingent claims and arbitrage-free pricing measures, with R plots showing constrained portfolios and shorting. This is a concrete mathematical bridge into later risk-neutral valuation. The document itself is dated September 8, 2020, illustrating that an OCW 2024 resource need not have been authored that semester. [One-period models PDF](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-642-topics-in-mathematics-with-applications-in-finance-fall-2024/mit18_642_f24_lec02_2.pdf)

The equal-weight portfolio exercise obtains daily prices for then-current S&P 500 constituents from January 2019 through August 2024, saves both all-stock and complete-data subsets, and plots portfolios, including Apple, Amazon, Netflix and Google. Instructions explain running and knitting R Markdown. As an analyst's methodological inference, selecting current constituents and excluding missing histories merits explicit survivorship/selection checks before interpreting an exercise as an investable historical backtest. The instructions establish a teaching workflow, not such a validation. [Equal-weight instructions PDF](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-642-topics-in-mathematics-with-applications-in-finance-fall-2024/mit18_642_f24_lec02_3.pdf)

The ETF case study provides executable-style R commands and results: daily data become weekly returns, sector returns are compared with index ETFs, and regression fits are diagnosed through residual and influence plots. It uses an updated 2013–2024 analysis period, with source data shown through September 19, 2024. Its focal consumer-staples example can be changed to other sectors. This makes regression a hands-on study of market exposures rather than solely a coefficient formula. [ETF case-study PDF](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-642-topics-in-mathematics-with-applications-in-finance-fall-2024/mit18_642_f24_lec06_4.pdf)

Hypothesis-testing notes separately cover individual coefficients, restricted submodels and differences between periods, then apply these ideas to GE's CAPM relationship. Their date is October 3, 2023. They explain how to test stability instead of treating estimated beta as timeless. [Regression inference notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-642-topics-in-mathematics-with-applications-in-finance-fall-2024/mit18_642_f24_lec08.pdf)

The volatility deck covers annualization, historical averages, rolling estimates, exponential weighting and forecast-error evaluation. It surveys high/low/open/close estimators, GBM, jumps, ARCH/GARCH, stochastic volatility and option-implied volatility. Its teaching value is comparing estimators and models under explicit assumptions, with attention to forecast horizons and out-of-sample performance. Volatility modeling is continuity with 2013, not a newly invented 2024 subject. [Volatility modeling PDF](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-642-topics-in-mathematics-with-applications-in-finance-fall-2024/mit18_642_f24_lec17_1.pdf)

## AI, crypto and student work: limits of the evidence

The biomedical lecture's title mentions AI, but its actual transcript focuses on financial engineering. Lo explains the financing gap between research and clinical trials, distinguishes parameterizable risk from uncertainty, and uses discounted drug-development cash flows and Sharpe ratios to motivate diversification. An illustrative 150-project portfolio assumes independent outcomes; he then discusses covariance and argues that distinct rare-disease programs can have relatively low correlation. Monte Carlo simulations and the BridgeBio business model connect the argument to financing practice. The energy extension proposes a portfolio of fusion-enabling technologies with stand-alone commercial uses, including vacuum and magnetic-confinement systems. The discussion therefore provides specific portfolio mathematics and simulation-based business design. It does not teach a neural-network architecture or AI biomedical prediction pipeline; AI also appears as a competing destination for investor capital near the end. Numerical examples, scientific claims and company results are statements within the lecture, not independently verified current facts. [Lecture 18 transcript, especially pages 4–15 and 21–22](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-642-topics-in-mathematics-with-applications-in-finance-fall-2024/18642-lecture-18-version-5_transcript.pdf)

For the event-market session, raw HTML inspection found no downloadable transcript or caption file and an empty transcript-link field. Its scope therefore remains limited to the verified OCW description: Mansour discusses founding Kalshi, regulatory challenges and the opportunity to trade and price future events. More detailed contract mechanics or mathematical models cannot be established from that description alone. [Lecture 20 video resource](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-642-topics-in-mathematics-with-applications-in-finance-fall-2024/resources/18642-lecture-20-version-2_mp4/)

There is no dedicated cryptocurrency lecture in the published 2024 calendar. There is nevertheless a concrete crypto reference: the R introduction includes BTC-USD among Yahoo data examples. The same PDF names data-collection packages, time-series tools, forecasting, regression, volatility and regime-modeling libraries, and shows a reproducible notebook/cloud workflow. This is limited cryptocurrency data exposure; it should not be inflated into instruction on blockchains, token valuation, DeFi or crypto market microstructure. [R/RStudio introduction, slide 4](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-642-topics-in-mathematics-with-applications-in-finance-fall-2024/mit18_642_f24_lec01_2.pdf)

The final-paper topic list demonstrates the range of student investigations: election volatility, land prices, sports-betting binary options, SABR/Heston volatility, Black-Litterman allocation, game theory, Markov chains, AI-assisted pricing/trading, momentum across sampling frequencies and financing NASA technology. These are project titles rather than proof that an instructor supplied full modules on every subject. Their presentations are withheld from OCW. ## Differences from 2013 and how to use both offerings

The 2013 calendar also has 26 numbered lectures, but they are topical instruction rather than including the four 2024 presentation sessions. It separately names portfolio theory, factor modeling and three time-series lectures. It also lists specialist applications involving commodities, option/probability duality, FX execution, quanto credit, HJM and Ross recovery. Those dedicated titles are absent from the 2024 calendar; absence does not establish that every related idea disappeared from all notes. Conversely, the 2024 named equity, PCA, biomedical, event-exchange, systematic-trading and ML sessions are not named modules in that 2013 schedule. Counterparty risk and portfolio management existed in 2013 and have changed emphasis rather than simply appearing for the first time. [2013 calendar](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-s096-topics-in-mathematics-with-applications-in-finance-fall-2013/pages/calendar/)

For self-study, use the 2024 weekly sequence for foundations and its refreshed empirical examples, add the public Hull/PCA/counterparty/rates materials for the clearest modern extensions, and return to the 2013 specialist lectures for depth in topics missing from the successor's schedule. This is an analyst recommendation based on the verified map, not an official MIT prescribed pathway. Students particularly interested in quantitative equity or systematic trading should recognize that the corresponding 2024 guest sessions have no accessible recording or lecture-specific notes on their weekly pages.
