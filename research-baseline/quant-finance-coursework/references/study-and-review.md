# Study and Review Workflow

## Module Contract

- **Purpose:** Build study plans, diagnose misconceptions, and support assignments or projects without confusing source coverage with mastery.
- **Load when:** The user requests tutoring, sequencing, hints, review, exercises, or project design.
- **Do not load when:** The user wants only a direct professional calculation with no teaching component.
- **Output invariant:** Match the learner's prerequisite state and requested disclosure level, then include a transfer check.

## Tutoring Workflow

1. Ask for or infer the target capability and the learner's current attempt.
2. Identify the earliest missing prerequisite or invalid inference rather than restating the whole topic.
3. Give the least revealing useful intervention: a diagnostic question, assumption check, counterexample, hint, outline, then full derivation only when appropriate.
4. Ask the learner to apply the method to a changed example. Correct repetition of the original steps is weaker evidence than transfer.
5. Distinguish mathematical misunderstanding, notation confusion, computational error, and unsupported financial interpretation.

## Suggested Sequence

Use prerequisites rather than lecture numbers as the primary structure:

1. Contracts, cash flows, bond mathematics, matrices, and probability.
2. Regression, inference, time series, volatility, factors, and PCA.
3. Portfolio choice, constraints, risk measures, and calibration stability.
4. Markov processes, martingales, Brownian motion, Itô calculus, and SDEs.
5. Replication, risk-neutral valuation, numerical pricing, rates, commodities, and credit.
6. A reproducible project with explicit assumptions, dated data, out-of-sample checks, and limitations.

## Assignment and Project Use

The source corpus maps nine 2013 problem sets, five 2024 problem sets, six 2013 case studies, group/final-paper work, and an investment observation exercise. It is not a solution manual. Use assignment topics to identify competencies and task shapes; do not treat source problems or close paraphrases as held-out evaluation.

Strong projects preserve observation dates and transformations, compare a baseline, state the contemporaneous information set, separate estimation from evaluation, and report uncertainty. Examples include factor/volatility diagnostics, portfolio sensitivity, multiple option-pricing methods, yield-curve/PCA studies, and range-based volatility comparisons.

## Misconception Checks

Look specifically for:

- Treating pricing probability as a physical forecast.
- Treating option price as raw expected payoff.
- Treating nonstationary levels as ordinary stationary data.
- Using smoothed latent states as if available in real time.
- Interpreting PCA directions as durable economic causes.
- Reading optimization precision as input certainty.
- Assuming more high-frequency observations over a fixed horizon necessarily estimate drift precisely.
- Inferring full instruction from a lecture title or student-project topic.

## Evidence of Learning

Use a new surface form, changed assumptions, an adversarial edge case, or a request to compare methods. Grade final correctness, assumption awareness, method selection, verification, interpretation, and calibration of uncertainty separately.
