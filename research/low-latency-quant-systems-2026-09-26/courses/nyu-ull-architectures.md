# NYU SPS — Ultra-Low-Latency Architectures for Electronic Trading

## Scope and public material

NYU SPS publishes a current continuing-education course description rather than open lectureware. It targets engineers and architects already familiar with Linux, servers, networks, shell tooling, FIX, and market-data protocols.

## What it contributes

The catalog explicitly joins application design, kernel tuning, real-time analytics, servers, networks, hardware acceleration, and FPGAs with business requirements and return-on-infrastructure analysis. This makes architecture selection a constrained system decision rather than a race toward the lowest component benchmark.

## Transfer to trading systems

The skill should compare CPU, GPU, FPGA, kernel networking, and user-space networking by workload shape, determinism, data-transfer cost, development/operational burden, failure modes, and measured end-to-end benefit. It should surface the cost of specialized hardware and avoid recommending an offload without a latency budget and deployment case.

## Limits

The public catalog supports a topic and decision taxonomy only. It does not expose lectures, assignments, thresholds, or enough evidence to prescribe a specific FPGA, kernel, or network configuration.
